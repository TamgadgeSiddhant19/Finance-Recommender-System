Reserve Bank of India Master Direction – Interest Rates on Deposits and Depositor Protection

Section 1: Statutory Framework for Commercial Bank Fixed Deposits

Commercial banks, Small Finance Banks, and Regional Rural Banks registered in India offer Term Deposits (Fixed Deposits / Recurring Deposits) subject to prudential operating directions issued under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

Tenure of bank fixed deposits ranges from a minimum of 7 days to a maximum of 10 years. Banks are free to determine interest rates based on Board-approved asset-liability management policies, with higher rates permitted for Senior Citizens (aged 60 and above).

Section 2: DICGC Deposit Insurance Coverage Mechanism

Subsection 2.1: Insured Ceiling Limit

Under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) Act, 1961 (amended 2021), each depositor in an insured bank is insured up to a maximum limit of Rs. 5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakhs) for both principal and interest amounts held in the same right and capacity across all branches of the bank.

Coverage extends to Savings, Current, Recurring, and Fixed Deposits. Deposits held in separate capacities (e.g. Individual Account vs Joint Account vs Sole Proprietorship) receive separate insurance limits of Rs. 5 Lakh each.

Subsection 2.2: Mandatory Claim Settlement Window

Under Section 18A of the amended DICGC Act, in the event of an RBI moratorium or bank resolution, depositors receive interim payout of insured funds up to Rs. 5,00,000 within a strict 90-day timeline.

Section 3: Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) and Form 15G/15H

Under Section 194A of the Income Tax Act, TDS on bank deposit interest is deducted at 10% if total annual interest income across branches exceeds Rs. 40,000 (Rs. 50,000 for Senior Citizens). Depositors with total taxable income below the basic exemption limit can submit Form 15G (Form 15H for senior citizens) for zero TDS deduction.
