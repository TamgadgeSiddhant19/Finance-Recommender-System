Government of India Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) Scheme – Reserve Bank of India Issuance Guidelines

Section 1: Sovereign Gold Bond Overview & Eligibility

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are government securities denominated in grams of gold issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India under the Government Securities Act, 2006.

Eligibility: SGBs are restricted for sale to resident Indian entities including individuals, HUFs, trusts, universities, and charitable institutions. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) cannot purchase SGBs on original subscription, though they may hold bonds till maturity if purchased while resident.

Investment Limits: Minimum investment is 1 gram of gold. Maximum annual limit per fiscal year (April-March) is 4 kg for individuals and HUFs, and 20 kg for trusts and similar institutions.

Section 2: Coupon Interest & Tenure Structure

Subsection 2.1: Semi-Annual Interest Payment

Investors earn a fixed interest rate of 2.50% per annum on the nominal issue value. Interest is credited semi-annually directly to the investor's bank account.

Subsection 2.2: Tenure and Premature Redemption

The total tenure of the bond is 8 years. Early encashment/redemption of the bond is permitted after the 5th year from the date of issue, exercisable on coupon payment dates.

Section 3: Statutory Tax Exemption on Capital Gains

Subsection 3.1: Complete LTCG Exemption on Maturity

Under Section 47(viic) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, capital gains tax arising on final maturity redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds to an individual is completely 100% exempt from income tax.

Subsection 3.2: Taxation on Secondary Market Transfer

If SGBs are sold on the secondary stock exchange (NSE/BSE) prior to maturity, long-term capital gains (holding > 12 months) are taxed under Section 112 with indexation benefits (prior to Finance Act 2024 updates) or standard capital gains rates. The 2.5% per annum coupon interest is fully taxable at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate.
