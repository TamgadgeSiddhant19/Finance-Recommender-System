# SEBI Circular: Categorization and Rationalization of Mutual Fund Schemes
# Organization: SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)
# Document Type: Circular & Guidelines
# Topic: mutual_funds
# Asset Class: equity
# Publication Date: 2017-10-06 (Updated 2024)

1. Executive Summary and Objective
To enable retail and institutional investors in India to evaluate and choose suitable mutual fund schemes without confusion across multiple indistinguishable products, SEBI established standardized categories and definitions for all open-ended mutual fund schemes operated by Asset Management Companies (AMCs) registered in India.

2. Equity Scheme Categories and Portfolio Mandates
a) Large Cap Fund:
- Mandate: Minimum 80% investment in equity and equity-related instruments of large-cap companies.
- Definition: Top 100 companies by full market capitalization listed on Indian stock exchanges (NSE/BSE).
- Risk Profile: High risk, suitable for conservative-to-moderate equity investors seeking stability and liquidity with long-term capital appreciation.

b) Mid Cap Fund:
- Mandate: Minimum 65% investment in equity and equity-related instruments of mid-cap companies.
- Definition: 101st to 250th companies by full market capitalization.
- Risk Profile: Very High risk, higher growth potential than large caps with higher short-to-medium term volatility.

c) Small Cap Fund:
- Mandate: Minimum 65% investment in equity and equity-related instruments of small-cap companies.
- Definition: 251st company onwards by full market capitalization.
- Risk Profile: Very High risk, suitable for aggressive investors with long investment horizon (7+ years).

d) Flexi Cap Fund:
- Mandate: Minimum 65% investment in equity and equity-related instruments across large cap, mid cap, and small cap stocks without static caps.
- Dynamic Allocation: The fund manager has full discretion to shift allocation between market caps based on valuations and macroeconomic conditions.

e) Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS):
- Mandate: Minimum 80% investment in equity and equity-related instruments.
- Statutory Feature: Mandatory statutory lock-in period of 3 years from the date of each SIP or lump-sum unit allotment.
- Tax Benefit: Qualifies for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act up to INR 1,50,000 per financial year (under the Old Tax Regime).

3. Debt Scheme Categories
a) Liquid Fund: Investment in debt and money market securities with maturity up to 91 days only. Suitable for short-term emergency fund parking.
b) Corporate Bond Fund: Minimum 80% investment in highest-rated corporate bonds (AA+ and above).
c) Banking and PSU Fund: Minimum 80% investment in debt instruments of banks, Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), and Public Financial Institutions.

4. Regulatory Protection and Safeguards
- All mutual funds are governed by the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996.
- Assets are held by independent custodians and overseen by Trustees to safeguard unit-holders' interests.
