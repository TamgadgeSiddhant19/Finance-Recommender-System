Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB) - Operational Guidelines & Scheme Features

Issuing Authority: Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India.

Eligibility: Resident individuals, HUFs, Trusts, Universities, and Charitable Institutions in India.

1. What are Sovereign Gold Bonds?

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are government securities denominated in grams of 999 purity gold issued under the Government Securities Act, 2006. They serve as a secure substitute for holding physical gold with sovereign credit backing.

  • Denomination & Units: Issued in multiples of 1 gram of gold (minimum investment: 1 gram).
  • Maximum Subscription Limits: 4 kg per individual and HUF per fiscal year; 20 kg for trusts and charitable institutions.
  • Tenor & Premature Exit: The tenor of the Bond is 8 years. Early exit options are available from the 5th year onwards on the interest payment dates.

2. Fixed Interest Rate & Redemption Price

Guaranteed Annual Coupon: Investors receive a fixed interest rate of 2.50% per annum on the initial nominal issue value. The interest is credited semi-annually directly to the investor's bank account.

Redemption Price: On maturity after 8 years (or early exit after 5 years), the redemption value is paid in Indian Rupees (INR) based on the simple average closing price of 999 purity gold published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) for the previous 3 working days.

3. Capital Gains and Income Tax Exemption

The Sovereign Gold Bond scheme offers distinct tax advantages compared to physical gold or Gold ETFs:

  1. Complete Capital Gains Exemption: Capital gains tax arising upon redemption of SGBs by an individual at maturity is 100% EXEMPT from Income Tax under Section 47(viic) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
  2. Semi-Annual Interest Taxation: The 2.50% annual coupon interest is taxable under the head "Income from Other Sources" at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate. No TDS is deducted on interest payments.
  3. Tradability: SGBs held in demat form are eligible for trading on Indian stock exchanges (NSE/BSE) and can be pledged as collateral for bank loans.
