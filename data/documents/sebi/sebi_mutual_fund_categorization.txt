## Section 1: Executive Summary and Statutory Objective
To enable retail and institutional investors in India to evaluate and choose suitable mutual fund schemes without confusion across multiple indistinguishable products, SEBI established standardized categories and definitions for all open-ended mutual fund schemes operated by Asset Management Companies (AMCs) registered in India. Only one scheme per category is permitted per AMC, except for Index Funds/ETFs and Sectoral/Thematic Funds.

## Section 2: Equity Scheme Categories and Portfolio Mandates
### Subsection 2.1: Large Cap Fund
- Mandate: Minimum 80% investment in equity and equity-related instruments of large-cap companies.
- Market Cap Definition: Top 100 companies by full market capitalization listed on Indian stock exchanges (NSE/BSE).
- Risk & Suitability: High risk, suitable for conservative-to-moderate equity investors seeking stability, steady compound growth, and liquidity.

### Subsection 2.2: Mid Cap Fund
- Mandate: Minimum 65% investment in equity and equity-related instruments of mid-cap companies.
- Market Cap Definition: 101st to 250th companies by full market capitalization.
- Risk & Suitability: Very High risk, higher growth potential than large caps with higher short-to-medium term volatility.

### Subsection 2.3: Small Cap Fund
- Mandate: Minimum 65% investment in equity and equity-related instruments of small-cap companies.
- Market Cap Definition: 251st company onwards by full market capitalization.
- Risk & Suitability: Very High risk, suitable for aggressive investors with a long investment horizon (7+ years).

### Subsection 2.4: Flexi Cap Fund
- Mandate: Minimum 65% investment in equity and equity-related instruments across large cap, mid cap, and small cap stocks without static minimum market cap constraints.
- Dynamic Allocation: The fund manager has full discretion to shift allocation between market caps based on market valuations and macroeconomic fundamentals.

### Subsection 2.5: Multi Cap Fund
- Mandate: Minimum 75% total equity investment, with a mandatory minimum of 25% each in Large Cap, Mid Cap, and Small Cap companies at all times.

### Subsection 2.6: ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)
- Mandate: Minimum 80% investment in equity and equity-related instruments.
- Statutory Feature: Mandatory 3-year lock-in period from the date of each SIP installment or lump sum investment. Eligible for Section 80C tax deduction under the Old Tax Regime.

## Section 3: Debt Scheme Categories and Duration Mandates
### Subsection 3.1: Overnight & Liquid Funds
- Overnight Fund: Invests in debt securities having maturity of 1 business day. Lowest credit and interest rate risk.
- Liquid Fund: Invests in debt and money market securities with Macaulay duration and maturity up to 91 days only.

### Subsection 3.2: Short Duration & Corporate Bond Funds
- Short Duration Fund: Investment in debt and money market instruments such that the Macaulay duration of the portfolio is between 1 year and 3 years.
- Corporate Bond Fund: Minimum 80% investment in highest-rated corporate bonds (AA+ and above rating).

## Section 4: Hybrid Scheme Categories and Asset Allocation
### Subsection 4.1: Aggressive Hybrid Fund
- Mandate: 65% to 80% in equity and equity-related instruments; 20% to 35% in debt instruments.
- Taxation: Qualified as equity-oriented fund for capital gains taxation under Section 112A.

### Subsection 4.2: Conservative Hybrid Fund
- Mandate: 10% to 25% in equity instruments; 75% to 90% in debt instruments. Suitable for regular income seekers.

### Subsection 4.3: Balanced Advantage / Dynamic Asset Allocation Fund
- Mandate: Dynamically managed investment in equity and debt (0% to 100%) based on algorithmic or quantitative valuation models.
